1. Tennessee fails to execute Christa Pike

Qué pasó

On the night of Sept. 30, Tennessee tried to execute Christa Pike, 50, at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville. She survived two doses of pentobarbital. Pike was sentenced to death in 1996 for the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer at a job-training program for low-income young people. Her accomplice, Tadaryl Shipp, got life because he was a minor. Pike would have been the first woman executed in Tennessee in more than 200 years. Her lawyers said the IV team needed at least seven needle sticks, and the drug likely pooled in tissue instead of entering a vein. She was taken to a hospital and put on a ventilator. The Death Penalty Information Center says she is the first person known to survive receiving lethal-injection drugs.

Conexión con hoy

Gov. Bill Lee ordered an independent review and halted executions for the rest of 2026. That postponed the only other execution on the calendar. Corrections Commissioner Frank Strada is stepping down. Pike was moved from the hospital to the Debra K. Johnson Rehabilitation Center on Oct. 10. At an Oct. 7 hearing, the state said any new execution date is up to the Tennessee Supreme Court, and a judge ordered the evidence preserved. Released 911 recordings showed confusion inside the prison that night. Pike's first public statement came on Oct. 30: "There hasn't been a moment since that night when I haven't been in pain." Her lawyers are seeking commutation. There is a precedent for trying again: Alabama executed Kenneth Smith in 2024 after a failed attempt in 2022.

2. The G7 releases 100 million barrels of oil and diesel

Qué pasó

On Oct. 2, G7 leaders on a call chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and fuel through the International Energy Agency over four months. A first batch of diesel was to go out within 20 days. They also agreed not to restrict energy exports to one another. The deal came after President Trump threatened to ban US diesel exports; he later said a ban was "never really on the table." The shortage comes from Iran's restrictions on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz since the US and Israel attacked Iran. Brent crude, about $73 before the war, briefly dipped below $100 on the news and then settled near $102. UK diesel had passed £2 a litre.

Conexión con hoy

Prices did not stay down. Oil rose more than 10% in October. On Nov. 2, Brent fell 7.6% to $104.77 and US crude to $96.46 on hopes of US–Iran talks. Trump says Iran wants a deal. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian says he is open to dialogue, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi wants sanctions and the blockade lifted first. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump "hasn't made a decision" on new strikes. Hormuz remains the deciding factor for fuel prices this winter.

3. Sánchez calls a snap Spanish election over housing

Qué pasó

In late September, video of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal being evicted from the Madrid flat she had lived in since 1956 went viral. It set off protests across Spain. On Oct. 2, the right-wing opposition and Junts voted down two government housing decrees in parliament. On Oct. 3, a huge crowd marched in Madrid and protesters set up tents in the Puerta del Sol. On Oct. 5, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called an early general election for Nov. 29 and warned of a "far-right wave." That evening, a large housing march in Barcelona ended in clashes with police. On Oct. 6, the cabinet re-approved most of the rejected measures as 21 urgent steps, including eviction protections and a €10 billion fund for first-home loans. Polls at the time had the conservative Popular Party (PP) at about 34%, Sánchez's Socialists (PSOE) at 26% and the far-right Vox at 18%.

Conexión con hoy

The campaign changed on Oct. 28. A high-speed train hit a stationary train near Urda, in Toledo province, killing 69 people. Officials pointed to a failure in the old LZB train-control system. Rail safety and competence replaced housing as the main issues. Recent polls give the PP about 31–33%, PSOE about 25–27% and Vox about 19–20%. In some polls, PP and Vox together pass the 176 seats needed for a majority. The government plans to put its housing decrees to a vote on Nov. 12, the day after a general strike on Nov. 11 and the day before the official campaign begins.

1. Zohran Mamdani is elected mayor of New York

Qué pasó

On Nov. 4, 2025, Zohran Mamdani, a 34-year-old state assemblyman and self-described democratic socialist, won the New York City mayor's race. He got 1,114,184 votes (50.8%). Former Gov. Andrew Cuomo, running as an independent, got 906,614 (41.3%), and Republican Curtis Sliwa got 153,749 (7.0%). More than 2.2 million ballots were cast. Mamdani ran on rent freezes, free buses, city-run grocery stores and free child care, paid for by higher taxes on the rich.

Conexión con hoy

He took office in January. Some promises have moved. In June, the Rent Guidelines Board voted a two-year rent freeze for about 1 million rent-stabilized apartments. A pilot of five city-run grocery stores is still being set up. Free buses have been put off in favor of making buses faster first. The administration is pushing a tax on expensive second homes and has a 10-year goal of building 200,000 affordable homes and preserving 200,000 more. It closed a budget gap partly by restructuring pension payments. It has had fights with the City Council: it sued the Council over a paraprofessional bonus law, and by Oct. 23 Mamdani had signed just one Council bill and vetoed one, while 89 became law without his signature. Labor contracts with big city unions are coming up. Today, Nov. 3, he is backing Gov. Kathy Hochul in her re-election race.

2. The Supreme Court hears the Trump tariffs case

Qué pasó

On Nov. 5, 2025, the Supreme Court heard more than two and a half hours of argument on whether the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) lets the president impose tariffs. Solicitor General D. John Sauer defended the tariffs. Neal Katyal argued for small businesses, and Oregon's Benjamin Gutman for 12 states. Chief Justice John Roberts described the claimed power as one "to impose tariffs on any product from any country … in any amount for any length of time." Justice Sonia Sotomayor said: "you want to say tariffs are not taxes, but that's exactly what they are." Justice Neil Gorsuch warned of a "one-way ratchet" of power moving to the president. Justice Amy Coney Barrett worried that refunds would be "a complete mess." Justices Brett Kavanaugh and Samuel Alito were more sympathetic to the government.

Conexión con hoy

The skepticism held. On Feb. 20, 2026, in Learning Resources v. Trump, the Court ruled 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. Roberts wrote the opinion, and Kavanaugh, Alito and Justice Clarence Thomas dissented. Trump switched to Section 122 of the Trade Act, which allows tariffs of up to 15% for 150 days. Those expired on July 24. Tariffs under Sections 232 and 301 remain. Refunds are now being paid. Customs had collected about $166 billion in IEEPA duties from some 330,000 importers. By Oct. 2, its refund portal had accepted about $136.6 billion in claims and paid about $126 billion, including interest. The Court of International Trade has certified a class of importers who did not sue, and the Justice Department is appealing a separate refund order.

3. UPS Flight 2976 crashes in Louisville

Qué pasó

At about 5:14 p.m. on Nov. 4, 2025, UPS Flight 2976, an MD-11 cargo jet bound for Honolulu, crashed just after takeoff from Louisville, Kentucky. Its left engine and pylon came off the wing as the plane lifted off. The jet hit businesses beside the airport. The three pilots, Richard Wartenberg, Lee Truitt and Dana Diamond, and 11 people on the ground were killed. A 15th victim died of injuries 51 days later.

Conexión con hoy

The NTSB found fatigue cracking in a bearing race in the left pylon's rear mount, which led the mounting lugs to fail. At its hearing on May 19–20, 2026, it heard that there had been 10 similar bearing fractures over two decades, and only four were reported to the FAA. Boeing had extended the inspection interval and did not classify the problem as a flight-safety risk. The FAA grounded all MD-11s. UPS retired its MD-11 fleet and took a $137 million after-tax charge. FedEx resumed MD-11 flights after the FAA approved Boeing's fix. The NTSB's final report is expected within the next year. Louisville marks the anniversary tomorrow, Nov. 4, with bells at 5:14 p.m.

1. Glenn Youngkin wins Virginia

Qué pasó

On Nov. 2, 2021, Republican businessman Glenn Youngkin beat former Democratic Gov. Terry McAuliffe for governor of Virginia, a state Joe Biden had won by 10 points a year earlier. Youngkin got 1,663,596 votes (50.6%) to McAuliffe's 1,600,116 (48.6%). He campaigned on schools and parents' rights and kept Donald Trump at a distance. Republicans saw it as a model for winning suburban voters.

Conexión con hoy

The model did not last in Virginia. Youngkin could not run again, and in 2025 Democrat Abigail Spanberger beat his lieutenant governor, Winsome Earle-Sears, with about 57.6% of the vote. Spanberger was sworn in on Jan. 17, 2026, as Virginia's first female governor. Youngkin is now chairman at Red Cell Partners. In August he launched Empowering America's Parents to promote the new federal Education Freedom Tax Credit, which starts in January 2027. He is often named as a possible 2028 presidential candidate. On Nov. 2 he campaigned for Rep. John McGuire ahead of today's midterms.

2. The Astroworld crowd crush

Qué pasó

On Nov. 5, 2021, during Travis Scott's headline set at his Astroworld Festival in Houston, the crowd pressed toward the stage until people could not breathe. Ten people died of compression asphyxia, the youngest a 9-year-old boy, Ezra Blount. Hundreds were injured. Scott kept performing for a while as fans begged for the show to stop. Early claims that someone was injecting people with drugs turned out to be false.

Conexión con hoy

No one was charged. In June 2023 a Harris County grand jury declined to indict Scott and five others. The Houston police report, released in July 2023, showed that a worker had warned an organizer that "someone's going to end up dead." All 10 wrongful-death lawsuits were settled by May 2024, the last one by the Blount family, on confidential terms. Many injury suits were also settled. The family of victim Madison Dubiski runs the Pink Bows Foundation, which provides safe-space tents at concerts and crowd-safety training.

3. The House passes the bipartisan infrastructure bill

Qué pasó

Late on Nov. 5, 2021, the House passed the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act 228–206. Thirteen Republicans voted yes and six progressive Democrats voted no. The $1.2 trillion law included about $550 billion in new spending on roads, bridges, rail, transit, broadband, water pipes and the power grid. President Biden signed it on Nov. 15, 2021.

Conexión con hoy

Its five-year highway and transit authorization ran out on Sept. 30, 2026. A short extension keeps Highway Trust Fund money flowing to Dec. 11. But the law's advance appropriations were not extended. Regional development groups say that leaves a gap of about $36.8 billion a year, a cut of more than 15%, which hits programs such as Safe Streets and Roads for All. A House committee has approved a replacement, the BUILD America 250 Act, but it has not reached the floor, and the Senate has no bill. Another extension is likely.

1. The Cubs end a 108-year drought

Qué pasó

On Nov. 2, 2016, the Chicago Cubs beat Cleveland 8–7 in 10 innings in Game 7 of the World Series. It was their first title since 1908. Cleveland tied the game in the eighth. A 17-minute rain delay came after the ninth. In the 10th, Ben Zobrist doubled in the go-ahead run and Miguel Montero singled in another. Mike Montgomery got the final out on a grounder to Kris Bryant. Zobrist was named World Series MVP.

Conexión con hoy

The 2016 team held a 10-year reunion at the Cubs Convention in January 2026, and pitcher Jon Lester entered the Cubs Hall of Fame. Theo Epstein, who built the team, left in 2020. He now owns a minority stake in the Red Sox and advises their owner, Fenway Sports Group. Manager Joe Maddon is retired. Anthony Rizzo and Kris Bryant are no longer playing. This season the Cubs went 89–73 and were swept by the Padres in the Wild Card round, losing the last game on Sept. 30. The Milwaukee Brewers, who won the Cubs' division, went on to win their first World Series on Oct. 28.

2. The High Court rules that Parliament must approve Brexit

Qué pasó

On Nov. 3, 2016, three High Court judges in London ruled that Theresa May's government could not trigger Article 50, the formal exit from the EU, without a vote in Parliament. Lord Chief Justice Lord Thomas of Cwmgiedd said the government "does not have power under the Crown's prerogative to give notice pursuant to Article 50." The case was brought by investment manager Gina Miller, who said: "The result today is about all of us." Brexit supporters were furious. The next day the Daily Mail's front page called the judges "Enemies of the People." The press regulator IPSO received more than 1,000 complaints about it.

Conexión con hoy

The ruling held. On Jan. 24, 2017, the Supreme Court upheld it 8–3, and ruled unanimously that the devolved governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland had no veto. Parliament then passed a short law, and May triggered Article 50 in March 2017. Miller won again in 2019, when the Supreme Court struck down Boris Johnson's suspension of Parliament. She later founded the True and Fair Party. The case set the rule that ministers cannot remove rights created by Parliament without Parliament's consent. That matters again now that Prime Minister Andy Burnham says any fundamental change to the UK–EU relationship would be for the public and the next Parliament.

3. The Paris climate agreement enters into force

Qué pasó

On Nov. 4, 2016, the Paris Agreement became international law, less than a year after it was adopted. It needed 55 countries covering 55% of global emissions to ratify. That threshold was reached on Oct. 5, and the deal took effect 30 days later. The US and China had joined together in early September. UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon said: "Today we make history in humankind's efforts to combat climate change." The first climate summit under the agreement opened in Marrakech three days later.

Conexión con hoy

The US is out. Its second withdrawal took effect on Jan. 27, 2026. The UN Environment Programme's Emissions Gap Report, released Oct. 21, says current policies point to about 2.8°C of warming. Full national pledges would bring that to about 2.6°C, and 2.1°C in the most optimistic case. Holding warming to 1.5°C would need emissions cuts of 18% by 2030 and 45% by 2035 from 2025 levels. The report also says renewable energy has grown much faster than expected. The next summit, COP31, opens in Antalya, Türkiye, on Nov. 9, with Australia leading the negotiations. It starts the agreement's second global stocktake.
