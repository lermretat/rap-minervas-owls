1. The U.S., Denmark and Greenland sign a defence deal instead of a takeover

Qué pasó

On Sept. 22, at the UN General Assembly in New York, Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland's premier Jens-Frederik Nielsen signed an update to the 1951 agreement on defending Greenland. The U.S. gets to expand Pituffik Space Base and set up new defence areas at Narsarsuaq in the south and Mestersvig in the east. It also gets broad rights to move forces around the island. In return, the U.S. recognises Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to decide their own future. It came after more than a year of Trump pushing for the U.S. to take control of the island.

Conexión con hoy

The deal isn't in force yet, because both parliaments have to approve it. Greenland's parliament held its first debate in mid-October. No party opposed it outright, but the opposition party Naleraq wants a referendum, and no date has been set for the final vote. On Oct. 22, Denmark sent the deal to its parliament, which will debate it on Nov. 12 and vote on Nov. 19. Frederiksen, Nielsen and Trump all still hold the same jobs.

2. Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington buys time but solves little

Qué pasó

Xi's three-day state visit, the first by a Chinese leader in more than a decade, ended on Sept. 25. The concrete results were modest. They extended the trade truce reached in Busan in 2025 by two months, to Jan. 10, 2027. Each side agreed to lower tariffs on $30 billion of "non-sensitive" goods, and China agreed to buy at least 10 million tonnes of U.S. coal a year in 2027 and 2028. They also set up a channel to talk about AI risks. The U.S. said Xi had promised to stop helping Iran. There was no joint statement.

Conexión con hoy

The harder issues still aren't settled. Rare-earth supplies to U.S. industry are still short, chip export controls haven't changed, and Taiwan wasn't resolved. Negotiators are now trying to firm up deals before Trump goes to the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November. Xi is due at the G20 in Miami in December.

1. Thieves steal France's crown jewels from the Louvre in broad daylight

Qué pasó

On Oct. 19, 2025, four men used a truck-mounted lift to reach a balcony of the Louvre's Apollo Gallery. They cut through a window, smashed two display cases and escaped on scooters in a matter of minutes. They took eight pieces worth about €88 million, including jewels that had belonged to Empress Eugénie and Empress Marie-Louise. Eugénie's crown was dropped as they fled and recovered damaged.

Conexión con hoy

All four main suspects were arrested within weeks and are in jail awaiting trial. One was denied release again on Oct. 22, 2026. The jewels are still missing, and investigators fear they may have been broken up and recut. Louvre director Laurence des Cars resigned in February 2026 after admitting the outside CCTV was weak. Christophe Leribault, previously head of Versailles, replaced her. A parliamentary inquiry found "systemic failures."

2. Sanae Takaichi becomes Japan's first female prime minister

Qué pasó

On Oct. 21, 2025, Japan's parliament elected Takaichi, a hard-line conservative, as prime minister. She replaced Shigeru Ishiba. Just before the vote, the Komeito party ended its 26-year coalition with her Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), so she made a new deal with the Japan Innovation Party.

Conexión con hoy

She's still in office a year later. In February 2026 she called a snap election and won it by a landslide, giving her coalition more than two-thirds of the lower house. Her government passed several conservative laws, including one punishing desecration of the national flag. Relations with China have stayed tense since November 2025, when she said Japan could respond militarily if Taiwan were attacked. A scandal that forced out her farm minister has pushed her approval rating to a record low of about 49%. She's now trying to pass a cut to the food sales tax.

2. Sudan's army seizes power and ends the shared transition to democracy

Qué pasó

On Oct. 25, 2021, Gen. Abdel Fattah al-Burhan dissolved Sudan's civilian-military government, declared a state of emergency and detained Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok. That ended the transition that began after Omar al-Bashir was toppled in 2019. Hamdok was briefly reinstated under a deal with the army, then resigned in January 2022.

Conexión con hoy

The coup leaders later turned on each other. Since April 2023, Burhan's army has been fighting the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) led by his former deputy, Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo ("Hemedti"). Tens of thousands have been killed and about 13 million people have fled their homes. A year after the RSF took El Fasher, Amnesty reports ongoing killings and torture there. Burhan rejected peace talks this month. Hamdok leads Somoud, a civilian anti-war coalition that's lobbying abroad for a ceasefire.

1. Colombians narrowly reject the FARC peace deal, and Santos wins the Nobel anyway

Qué pasó

On Oct. 2, 2016, Colombians voted against the peace agreement with the FARC guerrillas, 50.2% to 49.8%, a margin of about 54,000 votes. Turnout was only about 37%. Five days later, President Juan Manuel Santos won the Nobel Peace Prize. The Nobel committee said voters had rejected one specific deal, not peace itself. A revised deal was signed on Nov. 24 and approved by Congress without another popular vote.

Conexión con hoy

The FARC did lay down its weapons, and more than 11,000 former fighters are still in the reintegration programme. But 501 of them have been killed since 2016, and the UN says other armed groups have grown from about 15,000 members in 2022 to about 28,000 now. Only about a third of the deal's commitments had been fully carried out by late 2025. Abelardo de la Espriella, a right-wing candidate, has been president since Aug. 7, 2026, and his approach to the peace court is under debate. Santos is 75 and still defends the deal. In Oslo in September, he called holding the vote "my mistake."

2. The "Access Hollywood" tape shakes Trump's campaign

Qué pasó

On Oct. 7, 2016, The Washington Post published a 2005 hot-mic recording of Trump bragging to "Access Hollywood" host Billy Bush about groping and kissing women without asking. Republican leaders condemned him and some called on him to quit the race. Trump called it "locker room talk" and apologised. A month later he won the presidency anyway.

Conexión con hoy

The tape didn't end his political career. Trump is now in his second term. The recording came back as evidence in E. Jean Carroll's lawsuit, where a jury found in 2023 that Trump had sexually abused her and later defamed her. On June 29, 2026, the Supreme Court refused to hear his appeal, so the $5 million verdict stands. It turned down his request to reconsider in August.
