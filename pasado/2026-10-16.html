1. The U.S. House votes again to limit the Iran war as costs pass $38 billion

Qué pasó

On Sept. 15, the House voted 220–204 to pull U.S. forces out of fighting against Iran, except to defend American or allied bases. The Congressional Budget Office put the Pentagon's costs at about $38 billion through Aug. 1, plus $2–3 billion more each month. It also warned that stocks of air-defense interceptors are running low.

Conexión con hoy

On Sept. 24 the Senate rejected a similar measure 50–49, so the war continues without new approval from Congress. Iran keeps hitting ships near Hormuz, and Trump has signaled no major new U.S. strikes before the Nov. 3 midterms.

2. NATO jets shoot down a Russian-made drone over Lithuania

Qué pasó

On Sept. 15, Italian Eurofighters shot down a drone that crossed in from Belarus, and it was carrying explosives. Lithuania's president said it was a Russian Gerbera drone, probably headed for Ukraine and knocked off course by Ukrainian signal jamming.

Conexión con hoy

NATO chief Mark Rutte held it up as collective defence working, and he's pushing allies toward cheaper anti-drone systems so they stop sending expensive fighter jets after cheap drones. Everyone involved is still in the same job.

Qué pasó

1. Facebook's worst week: Frances Haugen comes forward and the site goes down worldwide

Qué pasó

Haugen revealed herself as the whistleblower on Oct. 3. The next day Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp were down for about six hours because of a bad network setting, not a hack, and she testified to the Senate on Oct. 5.

Conexión con hoy

What she said held up, but hopes for quick federal child-safety laws didn't. In 2026 Meta settled with most U.S. states for up to about $18 billion. Haugen lives in Puerto Rico, runs a nonprofit called Beyond the Screen, and is still campaigning.

2. Southeast Asian leaders shut Myanmar's coup leader out of their summit

Qué pasó

On Oct. 16, 2021, ASEAN (the regional bloc of Southeast Asian countries) banned Gen. Min Aung Hlaing from its leaders' summit, which it had never done to a member's leader before.

Conexión con hoy

The civil war goes on. After disputed elections, he became president in April 2026, and neighbouring countries are starting to welcome him back. Aung San Suu Kyi is still detained, and the Red Cross confirmed in August that she's alive.

1. Iraq launches the battle for Mosul against ISIS

Qué pasó

On Oct. 16, 2016, Iraqi forces began the assault on the city where ISIS had declared its "caliphate."

2. The siege of eastern Aleppo peaks

Qué pasó

About 250,000 civilians were trapped, and the "humanitarian pauses" Russia announced did little. The rebel-held east fell in December 2016.

Conexión con hoy
