1. SpaceX's Starship reaches orbit for the first time

Qué pasó

On Sept. 28, Starship Flight 14 lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 7:48 a.m. CT and became the first Starship to reach orbit. It deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. One engine shut down early during ascent, and for a while SpaceX's livestream said the ship would not make orbit, but it did. SpaceX cut the planned mission of about 10 hours down to about three. The ship then made Starship's first deorbit burn and splashed down on target in the northern Pacific.

Conexión con hoy

On Tesla's Oct. 20 earnings call, Elon Musk said Flight 15 will fly in November. On that flight the launch tower will try to catch the Ship itself, not just the booster. Musk said success was "more likely than not" and would be "the first time that an orbital launch vehicle has been fully recovered." He wants weekly flights in the first half of 2027 and two a week by the end of 2027. FAA airspace planning shows a tentative Nov. 12 date, but SpaceX has not announced it.

2. Prime Minister Andy Burnham opens the door to rejoining the EU

Qué pasó

Ten years after the Brexit vote, Prime Minister Andy Burnham used his Labour conference speech in Liverpool on Sept. 29 and a BBC interview on Sept. 30 to say Britain should look at every option for its relationship with Europe. He listed keeping the current arrangement, a customs union, the single market, or going "all the way" to full EU membership. He said a referendum "wouldn't be the right thing to do right now." He also said a referendum pledge in a future manifesto was "possible" because "where we are isn't good enough."

Conexión con hoy

He has since pulled back. Earlier this week Burnham suggested a referendum on free movement, and No 10 then appeared to backtrack. At Prime Minister's Questions on Oct. 28 he said there was "no confusion at all." He said the 2024 manifesto red lines (no free movement, no single market, no customs union) "will stay in place firmly for the whole of this Parliament." Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch accused him of not respecting the 2016 result. In a letter, Burnham told ministers to be "aligned and disciplined." The letter says any fundamental change "would be for the public to decide and a matter for the next Parliament." A UK–EU summit in Brussels on Nov. 20 is due to cover youth mobility, food and drink, energy and illegal migration. The youth-mobility scheme may be delayed.

3. A flydubai co-pilot attacks his captain with a crash axe

Qué pasó

On Sept. 30, flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv was carrying 174 passengers. Its 29-year-old Omani co-pilot attacked the Indian captain, Smit Machchhar, with the cockpit crash axe, and the plane dropped about 17,000 feet. A pilot who was rostered for the return flight noticed the cockpit door opening and closing oddly. He went in using the access code, and with cabin crew and passengers he subdued the attacker. The return-flight crew then landed the plane in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. Saudi Arabia arrested the co-pilot and handed him to the UAE, which leads the criminal investigation. Saudi Arabia opened its own safety probe. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu said the co-pilot apparently tried to crash the plane.

Conexión con hoy

On Oct. 9, UAE Attorney-General Hamad Saif Al Shamsi said the suspect had planned a suicide attack on the passenger terminal at Ben Gurion Airport. He said the man's radicalisation dated to 2017, when he was studying in Melbourne, and that he had used two earlier Tel Aviv flights to scout the target. The suspect flew for Oman Air from 2020 to 2024 and for Royal Air Maroc in 2025. He joined flydubai in January 2026 after passing its checks. He confessed to acting alone, and investigators are still checking that claim. Officials said the captain's head injuries could have been fatal. As of Oct. 21, Dubai–Tel Aviv flights were suspended until at least Nov. 15, with no bookings open before year-end. Israel wants its own security staff and X-ray screening in Dubai. Its Transportation Ministry admitted it had never vetted foreign crews. Etihad is now the only UAE airline flying to Israel, with six flights a day.

1. Rio de Janeiro's deadliest police raid

Qué pasó

On Oct. 28, 2025, about 2,500 police officers went into the Complexo da Penha and Complexo do Alemão favelas to serve 100 arrest warrants against the Comando Vermelho gang. About 122 people died, five of them police officers (the fifth officer died on Nov. 22). It was the deadliest one-day police operation on record in Brazil. Residents laid out 62 bodies in a square in Penha. Governor Cláudio Castro called the raid a "success." Police arrested 99 people and seized 122 guns, 96 of them rifles. A Quaest poll found that 67% of Brazilians approved of the raid.

Conexión con hoy

Human Rights Watch released an investigation on Oct. 28, 2026, the first anniversary. It reviewed 4,400 hours of police bodycam footage, and none of it shows a killing. HRW found signs that cameras had been tampered with, and only about 23% of officers were wearing one. HRW says 19 officers have been charged, with offences such as break-ins, theft and camera tampering, but none for unlawful use of force. Rio's Public Defender's Office released its own bodycam report the same day, alleging torture and electric shocks. Thirty-three of the people named in warrants are still at large, including the senior figures known as Doca, Gardenal and Pedro Bala, and the Comando Vermelho still controls the area. According to G1, shootings there have fallen 61%. A new governor takes office on Jan. 6.

3. D66 narrowly wins the Dutch election under Rob Jetten

Qué pasó

In the Dutch general election of Oct. 29, 2025, Rob Jetten's centrist D66 and Geert Wilders' far-right PVV each won 26 seats. D66 took 1,790,634 votes (16.94%) and the PVV 1,760,966 (16.66%). Finishing first by 29,668 votes gave D66 the first chance to form a coalition.

Conexión con hoy

The Jetten cabinet was sworn in on Feb. 23, 2026. It is a minority coalition of D66, VVD and CDA with 18 ministers. It is the first Dutch minority cabinet since 1918 to govern without a confidence-and-supply deal. The BBC called Jetten the youngest Dutch prime minister ever. His deputies are Dilan Yeşilgöz (VVD, also defence minister) and Bart van den Brink (CDA). As of Oct. 30 the government has no majority for its budget or tax plan. It is negotiating with JA21, BBB and SGP on the right and with PRO and Volt on the left. The tax plan goes to the lower house next week, with votes expected a week later.

2. The U.S. authorizes the Pfizer COVID vaccine for children aged 5 to 11

Qué pasó

On Oct. 29, 2021, the FDA gave emergency use authorization to the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine for children aged 5 to 11. The dose was 10 micrograms, a third of the adult dose, given twice three weeks apart. In the trial it was 90.7% effective at preventing COVID-19. The CDC recommended it on Nov. 2, 2021.

Conexión con hoy

The vaccine is no longer routinely recommended for all children, and federal vaccine policy is tied up in court. Under the CDC's 2026–27 guidance, updated Sept. 23, 2026, a COVID vaccine is recommended for immunocompromised children. For other children it is left to "shared clinical decision-making" between parents and clinicians. The FDA now approves Pfizer's Comirnaty for ages 5 to 11 only for children with a high-risk condition. Pfizer's vaccine is no longer authorized for ages 6 months to 4 years. On March 16, 2026, Judge Brian Murphy of the federal court in Massachusetts issued a preliminary injunction in AAP v. Kennedy. It puts on hold Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s appointments to the CDC's vaccine advisory committee (ACIP) and all of that committee's votes. It also halts HHS's Jan. 5, 2026 childhood vaccine schedule and overturns Kennedy's May 2025 COVID vaccine directive. The First Circuit heard the government's appeal on Oct. 6, and as of Oct. 21 the injunction was still in place.

Qué pasó

In Japan's general election of Oct. 31, 2021, Prime Minister Fumio Kishida's Liberal Democratic Party won 261 of 465 seats, comfortably above the 233 needed for a majority. With its partner Komeito, the coalition held 293 seats. Polls had suggested the LDP might lose its outright majority.

Conexión con hoy

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi called a snap election for Feb. 8, 2026, and the LDP won 316 seats on its own, a two-thirds majority and 125 more than before. Its coalition with Ishin holds 352 seats. Kishida chairs a ruling-party group that advised on Takaichi's ¥370 trillion growth strategy (Japan Times, Aug. 6). He has joined Oxford's Blavatnik World Leaders Circle, and on Oct. 20 he became an international adviser to the Praemium Imperiale arts prize.

1. South Korea's Choi Soon-sil scandal breaks

Qué pasó

On Oct. 24, 2016, the broadcaster JTBC reported that Choi Soon-sil had received, and sometimes edited, drafts of President Park Geun-hye's speeches. Choi was a longtime friend of Park and held no government post. JTBC said it had recovered about 200 files from a computer in Choi's office, including 44 presidential speeches and remarks. The next day Park apologized on television. She admitted that Choi had helped with her campaign speeches and some materials after she took office, but said this stopped once her Blue House staff was in place. Choi came back from Germany and appeared before prosecutors on Oct. 31.

Conexión con hoy

The scandal brought down Park, who became the first South Korean president removed from office. She was later sentenced to a combined 22 years. After serving 4 years and 9 months, she was freed under a special pardon from President Moon Jae-in on Dec. 31, 2021. Now 74, she has returned to politics. She campaigned for People Power Party candidates before the June 3, 2026 local elections. In August she attended a PPP retreat, her first since the party expelled her in 2017. Choi, now 70 and known as Choi Seo-won, received a combined 21 years for the corruption case and for a university admissions case involving her daughter. She is the only central figure in the scandal still serving her sentence, which is due to end in 2038. In June she was granted her third temporary suspension of imprisonment for serious health problems after spinal surgery. In September prosecutors extended it by two months, with her residence restricted to a Seoul hospital. In her first interview in 10 years she said: "I wish I had never met former President Park Geun-hye."

2. AT&T agrees to buy Time Warner

Qué pasó

On Oct. 22, 2016, AT&T agreed to buy Time Warner, owner of HBO, CNN and Warner Bros., for $107.50 a share, half in cash and half in AT&T stock. That valued Time Warner's equity at $85.4bn, or $108.7bn including its net debt. Both boards approved the deal unanimously, but it still needed shareholder and regulatory approval.

Conexión con hoy

The deal survived the courts but did not last. The Justice Department sued to block it. On June 12, 2018, U.S. District Judge Richard Leon rejected that challenge, and the merger went ahead. AT&T then reversed course: on April 8, 2022, it spun off WarnerMedia and merged it with Discovery to form Warner Bros. Discovery. AT&T received $40.4bn in cash, and its shareholders got 71% of the new company. On Oct. 6, 2026, Paramount, controlled by the Ellison family and RedBird Capital, completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery. WBD shareholders received about $31.02 a share in cash. The combined company is named Skydance and trades as SKYD. It owns CBS, HBO, CNN, CBS News, Warner Bros. and Paramount, has close to $70bn in annual revenue, and is targeting at least $6bn in savings. The Ellison family and RedBird hold 100% of the voting shares.

3. The EU and Canada sign CETA after Wallonia's blockade

Qué pasó

On Oct. 30, 2016, Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau and EU leaders Donald Tusk, Jean-Claude Juncker and Robert Fico signed the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) in Brussels. The deal had taken seven years to negotiate. The signing was days late because Belgium's Wallonia region had blocked it, and Belgium's regions approved it only at the last minute.

Conexión con hoy

Ten years on, CETA is still not fully in force, even as the EU and Canada move closer. Most of the deal has applied provisionally since 2017, removing tariffs on 98% of goods. The European Commission says trade in goods and services rose 80% between 2016 and 2025, to €130bn. But nine member states have not ratified it: Belgium, Bulgaria, Cyprus, France, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy and Poland. Slovenia ratified on Oct. 1, 2026. France's Senate rejected the deal in 2024. Italy has been stuck since 2018, and an Italian MEP said it was unlikely to go to parliament before the December 2027 elections. The investment court system will start only after all member states ratify. In September, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed moving "from CETA to an Alliance for the Future" and floated making Canada the EU's first "associate member." At this week's EU–Canada summit in Montreal, Germany and other member states had the term "associate membership" removed from the joint statement. The statement now speaks of an "ever closer association" short of full membership.
