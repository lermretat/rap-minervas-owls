1. Flávio Bolsonaro leads Lula in Brazil's first round; a runoff looks likely

With about 65% of polling stations counted Sunday evening, Flávio Bolsonaro had 49.58% to Lula's 42.25%, and Datafolha projected a second round on Oct 25 since neither candidate is above 50%. The lead upends final polls that had Lula slightly ahead, and the result will shape Latin America's largest economy and its relationship with Washington.

2. Iran says Hormuz stays shut until the US meets its seven conditions; more tankers hit

Parliament speaker and top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the strait won't reopen until Washington accepts conditions from the June Islamabad memorandum, including ending the naval blockade and sanctions and releasing frozen assets. The UK maritime agency reported two more tankers struck near Oman and in the strait over the weekend; the waterway has been largely blocked for seven months, keeping pressure on global oil prices.

3. De la Espriella announces undercover agents and an anti-corruption "search bloc"

In an open letter on X, President Abelardo De la Espriella said undercover agents are already working inside the public administration, created a "Bloque de Búsqueda contra la Corrupción," and named magistrate Cristina Lombana as presidential secretary of transparency. He also said contracts from the Petro government will be reviewed, citing a COP 14,984 million Ciberpaz contract with the Universidad de Pamplona.

4. Yemen's government launches an offensive to retake all Houthi-held territory

President Rashad al-Alimi announced a major Saudi-backed campaign to recapture Houthi areas, with Saudi Arabia expected to lead the air war; the Houthis answered with missiles and drones at Saudi Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais. It opens a new front tied to the wider Iran war, after the Houthis seized the Bab el-Mandeb Strait last month.

5. Army seizes a ton of ELN drone explosives as new drone attacks hit Tibú

Troops found a depot with 100 high-powered drone grenades, about a ton of explosives, belonging to the ELN's Juan Fernando Porras front in Sardinata, Norte de Santander, and destroyed it on site. Hours earlier, drones were launched at security forces in Campo Dos, Tibú, the second such attack in the municipality in a day; the same government balance reported the capture of "Salchichón," alleged head of Los Porteños in Barranquilla.

7. Government drafts rules on protests, a registry of Indigenous guards, and social-media listening

A Pentagon official told AP the Air Force flew home the bombers it had used to strike Iran from RAF Fairford, following an investigation into a planned terror attack on the base. The move comes as President Trump weighs new options, including more strikes, in the Iran war.

9. Medellín keeps night water cuts as El Niño drains Piedras Blancas

EPM will keep every-other-night cuts (8 p.m. to 4 a.m.) in parts of the northeast and center-east of the city this week, because the Piedras Blancas reservoir fell from 93% to 65% in a month and household savings are below target. Recent downpours haven't refilled the reservoirs, a sign of how El Niño is squeezing water supply even in rainy spells.

  • El Colombiano

10. Dodik's party claims victory in Bosnia's general election

Pro-Russian Bosnian Serb leader Milorad Dodik, banned from office, declared victory for his SNSD, and his ally Željka Cvijanović leads the race for the Serb seat in the three-member presidency; turnout was only about 42%. The outcome keeps nationalist parties dominant and complicates Bosnia's EU path.
