1. G7 to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel

After President Trump threatened a US diesel-export ban, G7 leaders agreed to a coordinated IEA release of up to 100 million barrels over four months, with a front-loaded diesel tranche in the first 20 days, and pledged not to restrict energy exports among members. The move aims to ease soaring fuel prices tied to the Middle East conflict and tighter global supply.

2. Colombia reverses October gasoline hike

On President Abelardo De la Espriella’s order, Minas and Hacienda scrapped the COP 46-per-gallon rise that had taken effect October 1, keeping the average gallon at about COP 15,886. The state will absorb roughly COP 8.3 billion in October to cover the ethanol-producer price update.

  • El Espectador

3. US sends a third aircraft carrier toward Iran

A US official said another carrier strike group and Marine expeditionary force are headed to the region, adding to roughly 50,000 US personnel already there as the US–Israel conflict with Iran enters its eighth month and Hormuz diplomacy stalls. Markets and allies are watching for escalation versus a possible opening on talks.

  • Al Jazeera

4. Eleven drone-and-explosive attacks on security forces in Chocó

Over three days, the Chocó governor reported 11 ELN-linked attacks with drones, grenades, and gunfire on Police and Army in Condoto, Nóvita, and Río Iró; one officer was wounded. Authorities convened an urgent security council as the San Juan subregion faces a new pattern of aerial explosive strikes.

  • Vanguardia

5. Brazil votes Sunday in a Lula–Flávio Bolsonaro showdown

Voters choose among presidential contenders with President Lula and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro (son of former president Jair) neck-and-neck; a runoff on October 25 is widely expected if nobody clears 50%. The race is a regional bellwether with Trump-era trade and diplomacy hanging over it.

  • Reuters

6. 61 arrests and 2,860 kg of cocaine seized in 24 hours

The government reported captures including Clan del Golfo members in Antioquia and Chocó and local gang members, plus hits on labs and illegal mining gear; 862 kg of that cocaine was seized in Valencia, Spain, in a container from Buenaventura. It fits a broader post–August 7 crackdown on trafficking finances.

  • El Nuevo Siglo

7. Kyiv mayor: intensified Russian strikes are “tearing the city apart”

Vitali Klitschko warned after crisis talks that attacks on critical infrastructure, housing, and logistics have put the capital in a “very dramatic situation,” including repeated hits on the Southern bridge that halted metro and road traffic. The strikes sit in a cycle of Ukraine hitting Russian energy assets and Russia answering against Ukrainian cities.

  • BBC

8. Government revives Plan Meteoro for cargo-road security

Transport Minister Elsa Noguera told Colfecar’s congress the state will restore heavy Police and Army presence on main freight corridors, with a tender already open for armored vehicles, motorbikes, and gear. Carriers cited 273 security incidents over four years, including burned trucks and killings, concentrated in Cauca, Antioquia, Norte de Santander, Chocó, and Cesar.

  • Caracol Radio

9. Ethiopia and Eritrea sever diplomatic ties

Addis Ababa closed its Asmara embassy and expelled 10 Eritrean diplomats; Asmara cut all diplomatic relations in response, amid renewed northern fighting and mutual accusations of backing armed groups. The rupture is the worst since the 2018 peace deal and raises fears of a wider Horn of Africa conflict.

  • BBC

10. Constitutional Court orders Nueva EPS to stop blocking medicines

In a tutela for a 10-year-old with an orphan disease, the Court told Nueva EPS not to impose bureaucratic delays that block timely medicines, calling prior barriers a form of institutional violence. The ruling sets a clear line for health providers on continuous access to treatment.
