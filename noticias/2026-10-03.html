1. Two oil tankers hit in Hormuz as Iran braces for a bigger fight

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported “unidentified projectiles” striking two oil tankers in separate incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, while a Tehran source said Iran is preparing for a “major round of fighting” with the US as Hormuz diplomacy stalls and a third US carrier group heads toward the region. The waterway carries a large share of global oil, so any sustained disruption feeds fuel prices worldwide.

  • The National

2. Explosive drones wound four civilians in Tibú, including a 14-year-old

In the early hours of Saturday, drones carrying explosives hit the Versalles corregimiento in Tibú, Norte de Santander, damaging shops and homes; victims’ advocates said three adults and a 14-year-old student were hurt and taken to Tibú’s hospital. Locals fled into the streets so attackers would see them as civilians; the mayor and the Defensoría have flagged a rising pattern of drone terror in Catatumbo.

3. Houthis claim strike on Riyadh oil facility as Saudis hit Sanaa

Yemen’s Houthis said they hit an Aramco oil site near Riyadh with missiles and drones; an AFP journalist saw flames and smoke at the Riyadh refinery south of the capital. Houthi media also accused Saudi aircraft of dozens of strikes on Sanaa as fighting around Yemen intensifies alongside the wider Iran confrontation.

4. Russia says Kyiv strikes will continue and urges diplomats to leave

Moscow’s foreign ministry said it will keep “targeted” strikes on Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities and warned foreign nationals and embassy staff against staying or traveling there, after recent hits on infrastructure including a Dnipro bridge. The warning lands as the capital’s roughly three million residents face another hard winter under stepped-up attacks.

5. Army kills Clan del Golfo chief “Terry”; 55 arrests and a ton of cocaine seized

President Abelardo De la Espriella reported that alias Terry, a Clan del Golfo leader accused of selective killings and extortion, was killed with two others in Jericó, Antioquia, in Operation Valkiria II. The same 24-hour security balance included 55 arrests, 1,011 kg of cocaine, 24 firearms, and seizures in Nariño; he also honored a soldier killed in a drone attack in rural Neiva.

6. Brazil’s Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro close campaigns ahead of Sunday’s vote

On the eve of the Oct 4 first round, President Lula marched through São Paulo and Flávio Bolsonaro ran motorcycle caravans and rallies, each predicting victory while polls still point to a likely runoff on Oct 25. Latin America’s largest election is a regional bellwether after Jair Bolsonaro’s coup-plot conviction and Flávio’s pledge to pardon him.

7. Israel strikes Gaza apartment targeting a top Hamas leader; five dead

On the Jewish-calendar anniversary of Oct 7, 2023, Israel said it tried to kill Hamas’s top leader in Gaza in a pre-dawn strike on a Rimal apartment; Gaza hospital officials said five people died, four of them women, and others were wounded. 8. Colombia swaps COP 19.8 trillion of debt to ease 2026–27 repayments

Hacienda completed the first TES exchange of a three-window reprofiling program, cutting scheduled amortizations by about COP 2.4 trillion in 2026 and COP 17.4 trillion in 2027 without raising net debt. Investors took longer maturities out to 2062 as public debt risks finishing 2026 above 66% of GDP and 2027 financing needs look heavy.

  • El Tiempo

9. Soldier killed in drone attack on Army troops near Neiva

In rural Aipecito, Neiva (Huila), explosive drones struck troops from Artillery Battalion No. 9; professional soldier Samuel Pinzón Barliza Ipuana died of his wounds and another soldier was injured by shrapnel. The Army blamed the Ismael Ruiz structure of the Central Bloc (Mordisco faction), part of a wider surge in drone attacks on forces and civilians.

  • El Tiempo

10. BanRep and Hacienda back keeping rates high amid a fiscal squeeze

Banco de la República governor Leonardo Villar backed keeping a restrictive policy with the policy rate at 12.25%, citing minimum-wage pressures and unsustainable debt, while Hacienda’s vice minister said the primary deficit is “not tolerable” and the budget already cuts about COP 17 trillion versus an unreformed path. The joint message: spending must fall before rates can ease.
